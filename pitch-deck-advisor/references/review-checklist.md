# Review Checklist & Anti-Patterns

Use this when auditing a deck (Review mode) and as a final self-check after
creating one. Structure the review report as: **3-minute test → critical red
flags → structural gaps → form issues**, each finding with a concrete fix.

## The 3-minute test

A person unfamiliar with the project reads the deck for 3–4 minutes without
commentary. Can they retell:

1. What you do
2. For whom
3. Why now
4. What proves it (traction/evidence)
5. How much you're asking

Report which of the five fail — these are the highest-priority fixes.

## Content checklist

- [ ] All 12 canonical blocks present (or absence consciously justified)
- [ ] Title carries the company one-liner
- [ ] Problem stated in the customer's world, with the cost of the pain
- [ ] Why Now answers "why not earlier, why not later"
- [ ] Market sized bottom-up (SAM = customers × contract value, arithmetic shown)
- [ ] Traction chart present (or signal evidence for pre-seed)
- [ ] Exactly one business model
- [ ] Competitors named, advantage durable (IP/data/network/switching costs)
- [ ] Team slide shows founder-market fit, not a photo gallery
- [ ] Ask: one number + milestones, no valuation
- [ ] Deep tech: IP/moat slide; proven-vs-to-prove split; risks named
      explicitly; CAPEX financing blended (see deep-tech.md)

## Form checklist

- [ ] 10–15 slides in the core; detail moved to appendix
- [ ] ≤ ~50 words per slide; one idea per slide
- [ ] Large type, high contrast, simple typeface (the "back row" test)
- [ ] Every slide parseable in ≤ 30 seconds
- [ ] Slide headlines are assertions ("X loses $Y to Z"), not labels ("Problem")
- [ ] PDF format; page numbers; "Private & Confidential" footer if desired
- [ ] Separate "read me" / "listen to me" versions exist (or is planned)

## Stop-list — automatic critical flags

Each of these is a critical finding regardless of anything else in the deck:

| # | Red flag | Why it kills |
|---|----------|--------------|
| 1 | NDA request before viewing | Marks founder as novice; investors don't sign pitch-stage NDAs (deal flow + legal risk investing in similar companies). Fix: "Confidential" footer, staged disclosure, secrets only post-investment. |
| 2 | Exit-strategy slide (early stage) | Investors know exits better; best companies are bought, not sold; predicted acquirers drive bad product decisions. Fix: delete; mention an obvious strategic acquirer on the competition slide at most. |
| 3 | "We have no competitors" | Market unresearched or problem not worth solving. Fix: include status quo (Excel, manual work, doing nothing) as competition. |
| 4 | Valuation in the deck | Valuation is negotiation, not presentation; an inflated number ends the conversation. Fix: "the market will set a fair price." |
| 5 | Multiple business models "to choose" | Reads as "we don't know how we'll make money." Fix: one model now, others as future potential. |
| 6 | Unrealistic projections | "$1B ARR in 5 years" without grounding undermines every other number. Fix: 3-year projections tied to established drivers. |
| 7 | Ask as a range / "open to offers" | Signals uncertainty. Fix: one number + specific allocation. |
| 8 | "18 months of runway" as rationale | Runway is not a plan. Fix: milestones — what becomes true in 12–18 months. |
| 9 | Vanity metrics | Signups/views/likes instead of revenue, retention, cohorts, NDR. Fix: one honest chart of the strongest real metric. |
| 10 | Top-down market only | "1% of $500B" without bottom-up. Fix: show the arithmetic. |
| 11 | Text overload | >50 words/slide, >30 sec parse time — the "visual essay." Fix: cut to one idea, move detail to appendix. |
| 12 | Small type / weak contrast / fancy fonts | Fails the legibility law. |
| 13 | Excess branding, animations, memes, subtle humor | YC's "distractions" blacklist. |
| 14 | >20 core slides | Sharp engagement drop. Fix: appendix or data deck. |
| 15 | Link with access request instead of PDF | Friction barrier; 40%+ open on phones. |
| 16 | Untranslated technical jargon | Especially fatal for deep tech. |
| 17 | Hardcoded numbers in the financial model (data-room stage) | Investors expect to play with assumptions. |
| 18 | Team slide = photos + diplomas | No answer to "why these people, this problem." |

## Stage calibration reference

| | Pre-seed | Seed | Series A |
|---|---------|------|----------|
| Most-read slides | Why Now, Team, Vision | Team, Traction (signal) | Traction, Unit economics, GTM scaling |
| Traction bar | Signal (pilots, LOIs, waitlist quality) | Early revenue / usage + retention | Charts up-and-right, cohorts, NDR |
| Why Now | Critical | Important | Barely read — traction answers it |
| Deck depth | Vision-weighted, 10–12 slides | Balanced 12 | Storytelling deck + separate data deck |

## Known-deck lessons (use as argument ammunition)

- **Airbnb (seed 2008)**: the clarity benchmark — one idea per slide,
  "explain to a 5-year-old." By modern standards it lacks Team and Why Now:
  forgivable then, mandatory now.
- **Uber (2008)**: strong pain proof, weak single narrative. Structure and one
  through-line beat quantity of ideas.
- **Coinbase (seed)**: brutal simplicity — one powerful analogy + two traction
  slides. A good analogy saves whole slides of explanation.
- **Buffer (seed 2011)**: led with traction; early revenue and unit economics
  covered a weak problem statement. Lead with your strongest card.
- **LinkedIn (Series B)**: deliberately detailed for sophisticated investors.
  Depth is calibrated to audience and stage.
- **Deel (Series A, no deck)**: with extraordinary momentum, investors pitch
  the founder. The deck is a tool, not a fetish — traction beats slides.
