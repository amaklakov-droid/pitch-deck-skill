# Deep Tech / Hard Tech Specifics

Standard "problem–solution–traction" logic gets extended for deep tech because
the investor is underwriting a different risk: not "will they buy it?" but
"will the physics/chemistry/biology work at all, and will it scale?" Apply
everything below **on top of** the canonical structure whenever the project
involves hardware, biotech, new materials, energy, novel science, or
manufacturing.

## 1. Start from the problem and market, not the technology

The classic technical-founder mistake is pitching "inside-out" (from the
mechanism), while investors evaluate "outside-in" (from the market). Science
supports the story; it never replaces it. State the problem in the buyer's
world with operational and financial consequences — never "current systems
are inefficient."

## 2. Separate scientific risk from engineering risk

Deep-tech VCs generally accept **engineering risk** (technically feasible work
that needs execution) and reject **scientific risk** (a breakthrough must
happen for the product to work). The deck must make this legible:

- **Proven**: what data, prototypes, and pilots already demonstrate.
- **To prove**: efficiency, system performance, techno-economics, scale-up.
- **Name the main risk honestly**, let the investor decide whether to
  underwrite it, and follow with the mitigating factors.

A deck that blurs this line reads as either naive or evasive — both fatal.

## 3. Milestone-based derisking, not a growth graph

Money is tied to retiring specific risks, not to months of runway. The
deep-tech investor thinks in 18–36 month R&D stages:

> "This round gets us to pilot validation with partner X, proving assumption Y
> at commercial scale."

Structure the ask and the roadmap as a sequence of risk-retirement milestones.

## 4. Dedicated Technology Moat / IP slide

Mandatory for deep tech — this is the main asset separating the project from
"a SaaS wrapper with the word AI." Include:

- Patents: filed, granted, planned.
- Unique know-how and proprietary datasets.
- Entry barriers for followers.

Frame the technology's difficulty as an advantage (solved science doesn't
"roll back"), never as a burden.

## 5. Beachhead market, not the biggest market

The first market is not the largest segment — it's the one where the pain is
sharpest, the value is quickly measurable by the buyer, and qualification
barriers are passable. The goal of the beachhead is collecting commercial
proof, which unlocks the bigger segments.

## 6. Capital efficiency and modularity (2025–2026 expectations)

For hardware/manufacturing projects, investors expect a **blended financing
model** for first-of-a-kind (FOAK) production: equity + grants/subsidies +
debt/project finance. A deck where the entire first plant's CAPEX sits on VC
money gets flagged as capital-inefficient.

Show **modular scalability** — capacity added in units — instead of an
all-or-nothing plant.

## 7. Prove "translatability"

Investors fund founders who can translate between the lab and the boardroom:

- Don't compress the science into slogans (makes the story unserious).
- Don't substitute volume of technical detail for clarity.
- Powerful trick: video of working — and being repaired — hardware instead of
  a CAD render. It demonstrates engineering endurance and that the thing is
  real.

## 8. Realistic ask size

An **undersized** ask from a hardware company (too little money for the stated
production goals) is as much a red flag as an oversized one. Sanity-check the
ask against the physical milestones it claims to fund.

## Deep-tech review additions

When reviewing a deep-tech deck, check beyond the standard list:

- [ ] Deck leads with problem/market, not mechanism
- [ ] "Proven vs. to-prove" is explicit
- [ ] Main risk named honestly, with mitigations
- [ ] Dedicated IP/moat slide exists
- [ ] Ask tied to risk-retirement milestones (18–36 mo horizon)
- [ ] Beachhead market chosen for proof velocity, not size
- [ ] FOAK/CAPEX financing is blended, not all-VC
- [ ] Technical claims translated into buyer value
- [ ] Ask size matches the physical milestones (not too small!)
